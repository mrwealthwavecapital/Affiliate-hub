Prediction Markets for Beginners: How Contracts Actually Settle

Before you trade on an outcome — or take a referral credit for one — here is how the market itself works, in plain language.

What a prediction-market contract is

A prediction-market contract is a yes/no position on a future event: will a candidate win, will a team cover, will inflation come in above a number? You buy the "Yes" side or the "No" side. The contract's price is the market's collective view of how likely the outcome is — a contract priced near the top of its range means the market thinks the outcome is very likely; one priced near the bottom means the market thinks it is very unlikely.

Kalshi is a regulated prediction-market exchange. Polymarket is a prediction-market platform. Underdog Predict offers prediction markets in California through CFTC-regulated exchanges, including its own UDX exchange and partners. The exact products, markets, and rules differ by platform — this page explains the shared idea, not any one platform's rulebook.

How contracts settle

When the event's outcome is determined, contracts settle — the winning side is paid out and the losing side is not. The exact payout value per contract, the settlement source (who decides the outcome and how disputes are handled), and the timing of settlement are set by each platform's rules for each market. Read the settlement rules on the specific market before you trade it: two markets that look alike can settle differently, and the settlement source is the part that decides whether you get paid.

Two things to check on every market before trading:

  • What counts as the outcome? The market's own wording decides it — not the headline, not what seems obvious.
  • When does it settle? Settlement happens after the outcome is determined and confirmed under the platform's rules; it is not instant at the final whistle or the announcement.

Why prices move

Prices move because traders buy and sell as new information arrives and as opinions change. A contract's price is a live, traded view of probability — not a bookmaker's line and not a guarantee. When news breaks, prices move fast, and a position that looked good an hour ago can look bad minutes later. That is the market working as designed, and it is also how money is lost.

Fees

Prediction-market platforms charge fees for trading, and the fee structures differ by platform and by market. The October 2026 research used for this site did not include a verified fee schedule for Kalshi, Polymarket, or Robinhood Prediction Markets, so this page does not quote fee numbers. Check the live fee schedule on the platform you use — fees are the part of the cost that is easiest to overlook and hardest to win back.

The risks, plainly

  • You can lose money. A losing contract pays nothing. Only trade money you can afford to lose.
  • Referral credits are not cash. Kalshi and Polymarket US referral rewards are trading credit — the credit itself is not withdrawable, and only trading proceeds may be, under each platform's live terms. A $25 credit is $25 of trading exposure, not $25 in your pocket.
  • Rules change. Markets, fees, settlement sources, and referral terms all change. What this page says was checked in October 2026; verify live terms before relying on any of it.
  • This is not a sportsbook. In California, online sports betting is not legal. Prediction markets operate under a different framework (CFTC-regulated exchanges), which is why Underdog Predict is live in CA while an Underdog sportsbook is not. Do not confuse the two.
