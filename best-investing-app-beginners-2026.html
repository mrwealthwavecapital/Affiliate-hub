Best Investing App for Beginners in 2026: What Actually Matters With $0-$100

Short answer: with $0–$100, what matters is not the brand — it is whether you can start with what you have, whether you can buy a slice of an expensive stock, and how long the free offer takes to become real money. On those three questions, Robinhood is the easier start and Webull is the better deal only if you can deposit $100.

What a beginner actually needs

  • Start with what you have. If the app needs a $100 deposit to unlock its offer, a $20 starter is locked out. Know the trigger before you sign up.
  • Fractional shares. With $100 you cannot buy a whole share of many companies. Fractional shares — buying a slice — are what make a small amount useful.
  • An honest free offer. A "free stock" is a real thing, but it is usually about $5, it has claim and sell windows, and cash takes longer than the stock does. Anyone promising a big payout is selling, not explaining.
  • Clear claim windows. Rewards that must be claimed in-app within 30 days are lost if you forget them. Put the deadline on your calendar the day you sign up.

The honest takeaway

Neither app makes $100 into wealth. Robinhood is the easier first step because its bar is lower; Webull is worth it only if the $100 deposit is money you were going to put in anyway.
