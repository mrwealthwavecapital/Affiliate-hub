How to Check Unclaimed Property in California (Free, Official)

California holds more than $12.3 billion in unclaimed property — dormant bank accounts, uncashed paychecks, insurance proceeds, utility and rental deposits, stocks and dividends, safe-deposit box contents. Searching for it is free and takes minutes. This page explains the official route, what you need to file, and how long payment actually takes.

Disclosure: This page links to a $15 search kit. The search itself is free on the official site — you never need to pay to look. This page is not legal advice.

Step 1 — Search only on the official site

Search at claimit.ca.gov (the California State Controller's Office, also at sco.ca.gov). It is free, forever. California is not on MissingMoney.com — for other states, use unclaimed.org (NAUPA). Any site that charges you to search California unclaimed property is selling you something the state gives away.

Step 2 — Search every name and address variant

Search your full legal name, name variants (maiden name, middle initial, nicknames on old accounts), and every address you have lived at in California. Property is listed under the name and address the holder reported, which is often an old one. Search family members too — with their permission — since property is frequently held under a parent's or spouse's name.

Step 3 — What you need to file a claim

To search, you need only a name. To file, you need a government ID, proof of the address or addresses matching the property, and proof of your Social Security number. Gather those before you start the claim — a claim filed with the wrong address history is the most common reason for a delay.

Step 4 — How long payment takes

Simple single-owner cash claims typically pay in 30–60 days; complex claims can take up to 180 days. This is slow, conditional money — real if you are owed it, but not fast cash. If a claim is denied, the site explains why, and you can refile with corrected documents.

What counts as unclaimed property in California

  • Dormant bank accounts and uncashed paychecks
  • Insurance proceeds and utility or rental deposits
  • Stocks, dividends, and mutual fund shares
  • Safe-deposit box contents turned over to the state

The finder-fee trap

California caps finder fees at 10%, and you never need a finder at all — the search and the claim are both free on the official site. Anyone asking for an upfront fee to "find" your property is selling you the free search. The same applies to lookalike sites: if the URL is not claimit.ca.gov or sco.ca.gov, close it and start over on the official site.
