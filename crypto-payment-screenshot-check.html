Someone Sent You a Crypto Payment Screenshot? Check the Chain, Not the Picture

A screenshot proves nothing. It takes under a minute to fake one. A wallet notification proves nothing either. The only proof a crypto payment arrived is the public record on the blockchain — and checking it takes about two minutes. Ask the buyer for the transaction hash, paste it into the right block explorer, and run the three checks below. Do not ship, release work, or hand over access until all three pass.

Disclosure: This page teaches a public-record check. It never asks for your private key or seed phrase, and no legitimate check ever will — a block explorer never asks you to sign anything or connect a wallet to view a transaction. Figures and explorer addresses checked November 14, 2026.

Step zero: get the hash, and get the right chain

Ask the buyer for two things: the transaction hash (on Solana it is called a signature) and the network the payment was sent on. A hash from one chain pasted into another chain's explorer finds nothing — or worse, finds a different transaction. The explorers:

  • Base: basescan.org
  • Ethereum: etherscan.io
  • Solana: solscan.io
  • BNB Chain: bscscan.com

Paste the hash into the explorer's search bar. If the buyer cannot or will not provide a hash, that is itself the answer: there is no verifiable payment. A screenshot is not a substitute.

The 3-point check

1. The transaction exists — and succeeded

The explorer must show a transaction page for that exact hash, with a status of Success. On EVM chains (Base, Ethereum, BNB Chain) a failed transaction still pays gas but moves nothing — the status field tells you. On Solana, check the Result field: Success and Finalized. A hash that returns no result, or a transaction still unconfirmed long after the buyer claims it sent, has not paid you.

2. The recipient is your address, and the token is the real one

Compare the To address on the transaction — character by character — with your own receiving address. For token payments (USDC and the like), the transfer lives under the Token Transfers tab, not the main Value field: check that the transfer's recipient is your address. Then check the token contract address shown on that transfer against the token's official contract address (find it from the token's own site or a major price tracker, never from the buyer's message). Anyone can create a worthless token with the same ticker symbol — the contract address is what makes it the real token.

3. The amount matches, with confirmations

The token transfer amount must equal what you agreed, in the token you agreed. For larger amounts, wait for confirmations before releasing anything irreversible: a transaction buried under more blocks is harder to reverse, and on fast chains a few minutes of confirmations costs the buyer nothing. If the amount is short, in a different token, or the transfer is still pending, the payment has not completed on the terms you agreed.

Red flags that end the deal

  • No hash offered — only a screenshot, a video, or a "trust me, it is sent."
  • The hash is not found on the explorer for the chain the buyer named.
  • The recipient address differs from yours by even one character.
  • The token name matches but the contract address does not.
  • Status failed, or the transfer amount is less than agreed.
  • Pressure to ship "before it confirms" or to share your seed phrase to "release" the payment. Both are scams. Walk away.
